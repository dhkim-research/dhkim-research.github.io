About · through the window

Between what a meter reads and what a person feels.

Felt

I am an architect and lighting researcher. A room can pass every daylight metric and still feel harsh; a dim café can read as ‘inadequate’ and feel exactly right. My work sits in that gap — measuring light carefully with HDR imaging and photometry, and asking people what the light does to them.

Project 01 · 2013–2018 · UCL

Light & Emotion

How does light make a room feel? My doctoral work traced a psychological pathway from lighting quality to emotion and well-being — across cultures (UK and Korea), offices, cafés and London’s night streets.

Project 03 · 2021–2024 · EPFL

Visual Comfort & Visual Interest

Do beautiful sunlight patterns make glare easier to bear? In a real west-facing room, thirty people worked with the sun in view behind three façade patterns. Glare was forgiven when the scene felt pleasant — not because of the pattern itself — and today’s glare metrics overestimated discomfort in very bright scenes.

Project 04 · 2025–2026 · ETH Zürich

Solskin

An adaptive solar façade that learns. Dynamic façades are usually steered by room sensors and predicted comfort. Solskin moves toward person-centric sensing and learned, AI-based comfort — closing the gap between predicted and perceived.
