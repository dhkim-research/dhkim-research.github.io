About · through the window

Between what a meter reads and what a person feels.

Felt

I am an architect and lighting researcher. A room can pass every daylight metric and still feel harsh; a dim café can read as ‘inadequate’ and feel exactly right. My work sits in that gap — measuring light carefully with HDR imaging and photometry, and asking people what the light does to them.

I also like sensors — most of my studies begin by building or calibrating the thing that measures. Your cursor here is one: on the measured side it reads luminance.

Project 02 · 2022–2025 · EURAMET

HiDyn

Glare is easy to feel and hard to measure. A street light against the night sky spans six orders of magnitude — far more than one camera exposure can hold. In HiDyn, a European metrology project, we tested how far HDR luminance cameras can be trusted for glare and obtrusive light, and wrote the good-practice guides that feed the standards.

Project 03 · 2021–2024 · EPFL

Visual Comfort & Visual Interest

Do beautiful sun patterns make glare easier to live with? Thirty people worked with the sun in view behind three façade screens — stripes, bamboo and Voronoi. The pattern didn’t change how much glare they felt. How pleasant the scene felt did.

Solskin AI

Solskin is an adaptive solar façade: its panels follow the sun, making electricity while shading the room. Solskin AI, with ETH Zürich and Zurich Soft Robotics, uses AI to control it so that building energy, occupant comfort and each person’s needs are met at once.

I build the instruments I measure with.

I like sensors. Most of my studies start long before the first participant — with a camera that has to be calibrated, or a logger that has to survive three weeks on a windowsill. These are tools I wrote or built along the way. The code is open, so anyone can check the numbers.

Instrument 01 · software · 2026

hdrspace

Ordinary raw converters make pictures; hdrspace makes measurements. It turns a bracket of camera RAW files into one HDR image in cd/m², keeps the whole chain linear, and writes every step into the file — so each number can be traced back to the shutter that made it.

Measures
Luminance (cd/m²) · colour (CIE XYZ)
In
Camera RAW brackets, fisheye or not, with shutter, aperture and colour calibration
Out
Radiance HDR, every step in the header
Also
Glare (evalglare in C++), view visibility (HDR-VDP 3), perceptual maps, tone mapping
Code
macOS · C++ · GPL-3.0 · GitHub ↗
Instrument 02 · firmware · M5Stack PaperS3

AirQ-Logger

Up to ten small air-quality nodes talk over ESP-NOW to one e-paper logger, which writes every packet to a microSD card. Local logging comes first. Wi-Fi only when asked — it shares the 2.4 GHz radio, and a lost sample is worse than a late upload.

Measures
CO₂ · PM1–PM10 · T · RH · VOC · NOx
Nodes
Up to 10 M5Stack AirQ, over ESP-NOW
Out
TSV on microSD; optional HTTPS upload
Code
Arduino C++ · MIT · GitHub ↗
Instrument 03 · field kit

Room sensor kit

For field studies in homes: a few battery sensors that sit quietly for weeks. One reads light the way the body does — melanopic EDI next to plain lux. A small cube reads the six surfaces around it for radiant temperature. The rest log CO₂, particles, humidity and whether anyone is there.

Light
8-channel spectral → lux, CCT, melanopic EDI (CIE S 026)
Heat
Six-face radiant cube → mean radiant temperature
Air
CO₂ · PM2.5 · PM10 · T · RH
People
